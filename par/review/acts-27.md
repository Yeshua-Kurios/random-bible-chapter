## Checks, round 1

- **review** (gemini): fail
  - v3: In KJV, 'entreated' means 'treated' ('treated Paul kindly'). Modernizing it as 'asked Paul for permission' is a mistranslation that reverses the authority of the centurion. -> The next day we arrived at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends to refresh himself.
  - v17: 'Which' refers to the boat/skiff from verse 16 (not 'ropes'), and 'used helps' refers to using nautical support cables, not 'helped each other'. -> When they had hoisted it aboard, they used supports to undergird the ship; and, fearing they might run aground on the quicksands, they lowered the sail, and so were driven.
  - v21: 'Abstinence' refers to going without food (fasting, as in verses 9 and 33), not 'silence'. -> After a long period without food, Paul stood up among them and said, "Gentlemen, you should have listened to me and not set sail from Crete, avoiding this harm and loss."
  - v39: 'They knew not the land' means they did not recognize the land, not that 'they could not see land' (which contradicts finding a shore in the same sentence). -> When daylight came, they did not recognize the land, but they observed a bay with a beach, into which they decided, if possible, to run the ship aground.
  - v40: 'Committed [themselves] unto the sea' means casting off / leaving the anchors in the sea. Saying 'they set themselves out to sea' implies sailing into open water, which contradicts heading for the shore. -> And when they had cast off the anchors, they left them in the sea, loosened the rudder ropes, hoisted the mainsail to the wind, and made toward shore.
